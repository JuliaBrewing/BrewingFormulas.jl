Attenuation and alcohol

The relations between the original, real and apparent extracts and the alcohol content, after Balling, and the energy content of beer.

BrewingFormulas.apparent_attenuation — Function
apparent_attenuation(og, fg; eq = SugarGravityQuad())

The apparent attenuation 1 - AE / OE, from the original gravity og and the (apparent) final gravity fg, with the extracts in °P.

julia> apparent_attenuation(15u"°P", 3.83u"°P")
74.46666666666665 %

source
BrewingFormulas.real_extract — Function
real_extract(og, fg; eq = SugarGravityQuad())

The real extract RE = 0.1808 OE + 0.8192 AE (Balling), in °P, from the original gravity og and the apparent final gravity fg.

References

  • J. De Clerck, A Textbook of Brewing, Chapman & Hall, 1958, after C. J. N. Balling.
  • M. L. Hall, "Brew By the Numbers — Add Up What's in Your Beer", Zymurgy, Summer 1995, pp. 54–61 (Equation 11).

BrewingFormulas.final_gravity — Function

julia> final_gravity(1.050u"sg", 75u"percent")

source
BrewingFormulas.abw — Function
abw(og, fg; eq = SugarGravityQuad())

The alcohol by weight, from the original gravity og and the apparent final gravity fg, by Balling's relation ABW = (OE - RE) / (2.0665 - 0.010665 OE).

References

  • J. De Clerck, A Textbook of Brewing, Chapman & Hall, 1958, after C. J. N. Balling.
  • M. L. Hall, "Brew By the Numbers — Add Up What's in Your Beer", Zymurgy, Summer 1995, pp. 54–61 (Equation 16).
julia> abw(15u"°P", 3.83u"°P")
4.799551015591193 %
source
BrewingFormulas.abv — Function
abv([model = Balling()], og, fg; eq = SugarGravityQuad())

The alcohol by volume, from the original gravity og and the apparent final gravity fg, with the given ABVModel:

  • Balling: ABV = ABW × FG / ρ_ethanol;
  • SimpleABV: ABV = 131.25 (OG - FG), gravities in sg.

See the models for the references.

julia> abv(1.050u"sg", 1.010u"sg")
5.316690074810317 %

julia> abv(SimpleABV(), 1.050u"sg", 1.010u"sg")
5.250000000000004 %
source

BrewingFormulas.calories — Function
calories(og, fg, volume; eq = SugarGravityQuad())

The energy, in kcal, of a volume of beer with original gravity og and apparent final gravity fg: FG × V(mL) × (6.9 ABW + 4.0 (RE - 0.1)) / 100, with ABW in percent by weight and RE in °P: 6.9 kcal per gram of alcohol and 4 kcal per gram of real extract less 0.1% of ash, per 100 g of beer, as in the Python BrewQuiver.

References

  • G. Oliver (ed.), The Oxford Companion to Beer, Oxford University Press, 2011, entry "calories", as quoted in "Counting Calories & Carbs", Brew Your Own, https://byo.com/articles/counting-calories-carbs/.
  • M. L. Hall, "Brew By the Numbers — Add Up What's in Your Beer", Zymurgy, Summer 1995, pp. 54–61, Equation 22, gives a variant per 12 US fl oz, 3.55 FG (4.08 RE + 7.1 ABW), which also counts the protein.
julia> calories(15u"°P", 3.83u"°P", 330u"mL")
187.9616874164696 kcal
source
BrewingFormulas.ETHANOL_RELATIVE_DENSITY — Constant
ETHANOL_RELATIVE_DENSITY

Density of ethanol relative to water, both at 20 °C: 0.78924 / 0.998203 ≈ 0.7907, used to convert alcohol by weight into alcohol by volume. Hall (1995) uses 0.794, the specific gravity of ethanol at 15 °C, which gives ABVs about 0.4% (relative) lower.

References

  • Density of ethanol at 20 °C, 0.78924 g/mL: CRC Handbook of Chemistry and Physics.
  • G. S. Kell, "Density, thermal expansivity, and compressibility of liquid water from 0° to 150 °C", J. Chem. Eng. Data 20 (1975) 97–105, https://doi.org/10.1021/je60064a005.
  • M. L. Hall, "Brew By the Numbers — Add Up What's in Your Beer", Zymurgy, Summer 1995, pp. 54–61.
