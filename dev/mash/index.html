Mash

Temperatures and volumes of the mash. The heat balances take the specific heat of the grain relative to that of water, grain_heat = 0.4 by default, and neglect the heat capacity of the vessel.

BrewingFormulas.strike_temperature — Function
strike_temperature(target, grain_temperature, water, grain; grain_heat = 0.4)

The temperature of the strike water (a volume or mass) to reach the target mash temperature when mixed with a mass grain of grain at grain_temperature, by the heat balance T_w = T + (c_g m_g / m_w) (T - T_g).

References

  • J. Palmer, How to Brew, chapter "The Methods of Mashing", initial infusion equation T_w = (0.2 / r)(T - T_g) + T, with the ratio r in qt/lb, https://howtobrew.com.
julia> strike_temperature(67u"°C", 20u"°C", 15u"L", 5u"kg")
73.2779481394733 °C

BrewingFormulas.infusion_volume — Function
infusion_volume(current, target, water, grain; infusion_temperature = 100u"°C", grain_heat = 0.4)

The volume of water at infusion_temperature to add to a mash at temperature current, with the given water (volume or mass) and mass grain, to raise it to target: V = (T₂ - T₁)(c_g m_g + m_w) / (T_w - T₂).

References

  • J. Palmer, How to Brew, chapter "The Methods of Mashing", mash infusion equation W_a = (T₂ - T₁)(0.2 G + W_m) / (T_w - T₂), https://howtobrew.com.

BrewingFormulas.mash_volume — Function
mash_volume(water, grain; displacement = 0.67u"L/kg")

The total volume of a mash with the given water volume and grain mass, the grain displacing displacement per unit mass.

References

  • The default, 0.32 qt/lb (0.67 L/kg), is from "Calculating Water Usage", Brew Your Own, https://byo.com/articles/calculating-water-usage/.
source
BrewingFormulas.grain_absorption — Function
grain_absorption(grain; rate = 1.0u"L/kg")

The volume of liquid retained by a mass grain of spent grain, at the given absorption rate (usually from 0.8 to 1.25 L/kg).

References

  • About 0.1 gal/lb (0.83 L/kg) is the usual rule of thumb, e.g. in "How To Brew in a Bag", American Homebrewers Association, https://www.homebrewersassociation.org/wp-content/uploads/How-To-Brew-in-a-Bag.pdf; brewing software uses from about 0.7 to 1.2 L/kg. The default here, 1.0 L/kg, is in this range and should be adjusted to the equipment.
