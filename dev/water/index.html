Water

The chemistry of brewing water: the ions added by salts, alkalinity and hardness, Kolbach's residual alkalinity, the protons given by acids, and an estimate of the mash pH by a balance of charges. Concentrations are in mg/L (or ppm); alkalinity, hardness and residual alkalinity are in mg/L as CaCO₃, one milliequivalent per liter being 50.04 mg/L as CaCO₃.

BrewingFormulas.alkalinity — Function
alkalinity(bicarbonate)

The alkalinity, in mg/L as CaCO₃, of water with the given concentration of bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻, in mg/L or ppm), by the equivalent weights of HCO₃⁻ and CaCO₃ (the other carbonate species are negligible at the pH of brewing water).

julia> ustrip(u"mg/L", alkalinity(122u"mg/L"))   # mg/L as CaCO₃
100.0597548184083

BrewingFormulas.milliequivalents — Function
milliequivalents(x)

The milliequivalents per liter, as a plain number, of a concentration x in mg/L as CaCO₃ (alkalinity, hardness, residual alkalinity): one milliequivalent per liter is 50.04 mg/L as CaCO₃.

source
BrewingFormulas.residual_alkalinity — Function
residual_alkalinity(alkalinity, calcium, magnesium)

Kolbach's residual alkalinity, in mg/L as CaCO₃, of water with the given alkalinity (in mg/L as CaCO₃) and concentrations of calcium and magnesium (mg/L or ppm): the alkalinity left after the calcium and magnesium react with the phosphates of the malt, 3.5 equivalents of calcium and 7 of magnesium neutralizing one equivalent of alkalinity, RA = alkalinity - calcium hardness / 3.5 - magnesium hardness / 7.

References

  • P. Kolbach (1953), as given in J. Palmer and C. Kaminski, Water: A Comprehensive Guide for Brewers, Brewers Publications, 2013, in "The Power of pH", Brew Your Own (https://byo.com/?p=8960), and in MEBAK method W-030.01.900, "Residual Alkalinity in Brewing Liquor". The original paper was not found.
julia> ustrip(u"mg/L", residual_alkalinity(100u"mg/L", 50u"mg/L", 10u"mg/L"))
58.44182691724805

BrewingFormulas.acid_milliequivalents — Function
acid_milliequivalents(acid, volume; ph = 5.4)

The milliequivalents of protons, as a plain number, that a volume of the solution of AbstractAcid acid gives at the pH ph (of a mash, about 5.4), from its strength, density, molar mass and pKa values (the fraction of each acid group dissociated at ph).

References

  • The pKa values are from the CRC Handbook of Chemistry and Physics; the densities of the solutions are typical values, to be checked against the supplier's.
julia> acid_milliequivalents(LacticAcid(), 1u"mL")
11.489505404836624

BrewingFormulas.BASE_MALT_DI_PH — Constant
BASE_MALT_DI_PH

The usual pH of a mash of pale base malt in distilled water, 5.7, the default of mash_ph: a rule of thumb, from secondary sources. The distilled water pH of crystal and roasted malts is much lower, and must be given.

source
BrewingFormulas.MALT_BUFFERING — Constant
MALT_BUFFERING

The usual buffering capacity of malts, 40 mEq/(kg pH), the default of mash_ph: a rule of thumb, from secondary sources, consistent with the shift of about 0.1 pH per mEq/L of residual alkalinity at 4 L/kg reported by Troester.

source
BrewingFormulas.mash_ph — Function
mash_ph(grist, water, residual_alkalinity; acids = [])

An estimate of the pH of a mash, by a balance of charges: each malt of the grist resists moving from its distilled water pH with its buffering capacity, and the water and the acids push it,

Σ mᵢ bᵢ (pH - pHᵢ) = V RA - acid,

with the masses mᵢ (kg), buffering capacities bᵢ (mEq/(kg pH)) and distilled water pH pHᵢ of the malts, the volume V of water (L), its Kolbach residual alkalinity RA (mEq/L, from mg/L as CaCO₃, see residual_alkalinity), and the milliequivalents of acids (pairs acid => volume, see acid_milliequivalents, at the resulting pH).

Each malt is a NamedTuple (mass, di_ph, buffering); di_ph and buffering may be nothing, for the defaults BASE_MALT_DI_PH and MALT_BUFFERING, right only for pale base malts. The pH is returned as a plain number.

References

  • The model of a balance of charges with linear buffering, in K. Troester, "The Effect of Brewing Water and Grist Composition on the pH of the Mash", braukaiser.com, 2009, and A. J. deLange's notes on estimating the mash pH, as described in J. Palmer, "Exploring the effect of residual alkalinity", ASBC 2016 (https://www.asbcnet.org/events/archives/2016/proceedings/Documents/GC_Water_Palmer.pdf), which reports about 0.1 pH per mEq/L of residual alkalinity at 4 L/kg, as given here by a buffering of 40 mEq/(kg pH). The original papers were not found.
julia> mash_ph([(mass = 5u"kg", di_ph = nothing, buffering = nothing)], 15u"L", 50u"mg/L")
5.774935555422337
