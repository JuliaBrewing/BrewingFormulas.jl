Conventions and models

BrewingFormulas — Module
BrewingFormulas

Formulas used in beer brewing calculations: gravity and extract, attenuation and alcohol, refractometer corrections, color, bitterness, mash and boil volumes and temperatures, carbonation and yeast pitching.

All functions are stateless and work with Unitful.jl quantities, with the brewing units of UnitfulBrewing.jl. Arguments may be given in any unit of the right dimension, and results are quantities.

When several empirical formulas exist for the same thing, each one is a model, a singleton type passed as the first argument, e.g. utilization(Tinseth(), og, 60u"minute") or srm(Morey(), mcu). Calling the function without a model uses the default one, listed in DEFAULT_MODELS.

Conventions for the arguments

  • Gravities and extracts are accepted in °P, Brix, sg or gu, and must carry their unit: a plain number is rejected, since 1.050 and 12 are both plausible gravities. Conversions between sugar contents and specific gravity use the equivalence given by the keyword eq, SugarGravityQuad() by default.
  • Fractions (attenuation, alpha acids, moisture, ...) are accepted as percent quantities or as plain numbers, which are taken as fractions: 75percent and 0.75 are the same.
source

Arguments

  • Units: arguments are Unitful quantities in any unit of the right dimension, and results are quantities.

  • Gravities are accepted in °P, Brix, sg or gu, and must carry their unit. A plain number is rejected, since 1.050 and 12 are both plausible gravities, and so is a percentage. Results derived from a gravity come back in the same unit family:

    julia> final_gravity(1.050u"sg", 75u"percent")
1.0121194807789198 sg

julia> final_gravity(12u"°P", 75u"percent")
3.0 °P

julia> plato(1.050)
ERROR: ArgumentError: a gravity must carry its unit (°P, Brix, sg or gu), got 1.05; e.g. 1.050u"sg" or 12u"°P"
[...]

  • Conversions between sugar contents and specific gravity use the equivalence given by the keyword eq, by default SugarGravityQuad() from UnitfulBrewing.

  • Fractions (attenuation, alpha acids, moisture, ...) are percent quantities, or plain numbers taken as fractions: 75u"percent" and 0.75 are the same.

BrewingFormulas.DEFAULT_EQUIVALENCE — Constant
DEFAULT_EQUIVALENCE

The equivalence between sugar contents (°P) and specific gravity (sg) used by default, SugarGravityQuad(), from UnitfulBrewing: °P = 668.72 sg - 463.37 - 205.35 sg².

References

  • This quadratic fit is attributed to Robbins (1975) in "On the relationship between Plato and specific gravity", Brewer's Friend, 2012, https://www.brewersfriend.com/2012/10/31/on-the-relationship-between-plato-and-specific-gravity; the original publication was not found.

Models

When several empirical formulas exist for the same quantity, each one is a model: a singleton type passed as the first argument and selected by dispatch. Without a model, the default one in DEFAULT_MODELS is used:

julia> srm(10)
7.239807780133753 SRM

julia> srm(Daniels(), 10)
10.4 SRM
QuantityModelsDefault
sugar contents ↔ gravitySugarGravityQuad, SugarGravity (keyword eq)SugarGravityQuad()
hop utilization, IBUTinseth, RagerTinseth()
MCU → SRMMorey, Daniels, Mosher, DrueyDaniels, DrueyNoonanMorey()
alcohol by volumeBalling, SimpleABVBalling()
refractometer final gravityTerrillCubic, TerrillLinearTerrillCubic()

New models are added by defining a subtype of the corresponding abstract type and a method for it, without changing this package.

BrewingFormulas.DEFAULT_MODELS — Constant
DEFAULT_MODELS

The models used when none is given:

  • gravity = SugarGravityQuad(), the equivalence between °P and sg;
  • bitterness = Tinseth();
  • color = Morey();
  • abv = Balling();
  • refractometer = TerrillCubic().

BrewingFormulas.AbstractModel — Type
AbstractModel

Supertype of the models, the singleton types that select one of several empirical formulas for the same quantity, by dispatch. New models are added by defining a subtype and the corresponding methods, e.g.

struct MyUtilization <: BitternessModel end
BrewingFormulas.utilization(::MyUtilization, gravity, time; eq = SugarGravityQuad()) = ...

The sources of the formulas are given in each docstring and collected in the file REFERENCES.md of the package.

BrewingFormulas.Tinseth — Type
Tinseth()

Glenn Tinseth's model for the utilization of alpha acids, utilization = 1.65 × 0.000125^(sg - 1) × (1 - exp(-0.04 t)) / 4.15, with t in minutes: the product of a "bigness factor", accounting for the gravity of the wort, and a "boil time factor".

Tinseth's model was not derived for pellets; the usual increase of about 10% for pellets is a homebrewing convention, applied here through the keyword factor.

References

  • G. Tinseth, hop utilization page, http://www.realbeer.com/hops/research.html (the realbeer.com site has since been retired); also in N. Pyle's Hops FAQ, http://www.realbeer.com/hops/FAQ.html, the source cited by the Python BrewQuiver.
source
BrewingFormulas.Rager — Type
Rager()

Jackie Rager's model for the utilization of alpha acids, utilization = (18.11 + 13.86 tanh((t - 31.32) / 18.27)) / 100 / (1 + GA), with t in minutes and the gravity adjustment GA = max(0, (sg - 1.050) / 0.2), sg being the gravity of the boiling wort.

Rager published the utilization as a table of boil times, together with the gravity adjustment; the tanh expression is a curve fit to that table, as given in Norm Pyle's Hops FAQ.

References

  • J. Rager, "Calculating Hop Bitterness in Beer", Zymurgy, Special Issue 1990, pp. 53–54 (reproduced at https://bwelman.github.io/bier/calculating-hop-bitterness/hop-bitterness.html).
  • N. Pyle, Hops FAQ, http://www.realbeer.com/hops/FAQ.html (the source cited by the Python BrewQuiver).
source

BrewingFormulas.Balling — Type
Balling()

Balling's relations between original extract, real extract and alcohol: the real extract RE = 0.1808 OE + 0.8192 AE, the alcohol by weight ABW = (OE - RE) / (2.0665 - 0.010665 OE) and the alcohol by volume ABV = ABW × FG / ρ_ethanol, all extracts in °P.

The constant 2.0665 comes from Balling's mass balance of fermentation: 2.0665 g of extract give 1 g of ethanol, 0.9565 g of CO₂ and 0.11 g of yeast dry matter. For the relative density of ethanol, Hall uses 0.794; here it is ETHANOL_RELATIVE_DENSITY ≈ 0.7907, the ratio of the densities of ethanol and water at 20 °C, consistent with the Plato tables.

References

  • C. J. N. Balling, Die Gährungschemie, Prague, 1845–1865 (three editions).
  • J. De Clerck, A Textbook of Brewing, Chapman & Hall, 1958.
  • M. L. Hall, "Brew By the Numbers — Add Up What's in Your Beer", Zymurgy, Summer 1995, pp. 54–61 (the relations as used here, after De Clerck).
  • J. Šavel, P. Košin, A. Brož, J. Vlček, "Interpolation formulas for Balling's alcohol factors", Kvasny prumysl 66 (2020) 239–244, https://doi.org/10.18832/kp2019.66.239 (the mass balance).
source

BrewingFormulas.TerrillLinear — Type
TerrillLinear()

Sean Terrill's linear fit of the final gravity from the refractometer readings RIi and RIf (in Brix, divided by the wort correction factor) before and after fermentation: FG = 1 - 0.00085683 RIi + 0.0034941 RIf.

References

  • S. Terrill, "Refractometer FG Results", 7 April 2011, http://seanterrill.com/2011/04/07/refractometer-fg-results/ (cited by the Python BrewQuiver).

BrewingFormulas.TerrillCubic — Type
TerrillCubic()

Sean Terrill's cubic fit of the final gravity from the refractometer readings RIi and RIf (in Brix, divided by the wort correction factor) before and after fermentation:

FG = 1 - 0.0044993 RIi + 0.011774 RIf + 0.00027581 RIi² - 0.0012717 RIf²
       - 0.0000072800 RIi³ + 0.000063293 RIf³.

References

  • S. Terrill, "Refractometer FG Results", 7 April 2011, http://seanterrill.com/2011/04/07/refractometer-fg-results/ (cited by the Python BrewQuiver).
