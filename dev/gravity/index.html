Gravity and extract

Degrees Plato are the mass percentage of extract in a solution, and the specific gravity is its density relative to water, both at 20 °C. These definitions relate the gravity, the volume and the mass of extract, and give the volume of a wort from its water and dissolved extract.

BrewingFormulas.plato — Function
plato(gravity; eq = SugarGravityQuad())

The gravity (in °P, Brix, sg or gu) in degrees Plato.

julia> plato(1.050u"sg")
12.387625000000002 °P

BrewingFormulas.extract_mass — Function
extract_mass(volume, gravity; eq = SugarGravityQuad())

The mass of extract dissolved in a volume of solution with the given gravity, both at 20 °C: volume × sg × ρw × P / 100, where ρw is the density of water at 20 °C. This follows from the definitions of degrees Plato (percentage of extract by mass) and of specific gravity (density relative to water, both at 20 °C).

julia> extract_mass(20u"L", 12u"°P")
2.5115668025638263 kg

BrewingFormulas.solution_gravity — Function
solution_gravity(water, extract; eq = SugarGravityQuad())

The gravity, in °P, of the solution of the mass extract in water, given either as a mass or as a volume at 20 °C: P = 100 mₑ / (m_w + mₑ).

source
BrewingFormulas.solution_volume — Function
solution_volume(water, extract; eq = SugarGravityQuad())

The volume, at 20 °C, of the solution of the mass extract in water, given either as a mass or as a volume at 20 °C: V = (m_w + mₑ) / (sg ρw), with sg the specific gravity of the solution. This is how the volume of a wort follows from the water and the extract dissolved from the grains.

julia> solution_volume(20u"L", 2.5u"kg")
21.54110091391626 L
source
BrewingFormulas.hydrometer_correction — Function
hydrometer_correction(reading, temperature; calibration = 20u"°C", eq = SugarGravityQuad())

The gravity corrected from a hydrometer reading taken at temperature, for a hydrometer calibrated at calibration, in the unit of reading.

The correction is the difference, in thousandths of sg, of the cubic fit 1.313454 - 0.132674 T + 2.057793e-3 T² - 2.627634e-6 T³ (T in °F) at the two temperatures. The fit (R² = 0.999969, from 0 to 212 °F) is to the density of water from the CRC Handbook of Chemistry and Physics, relative to its value at 59 °F; taking differences allows any calibration temperature.

References

  • C. Lyons, "Hydrometer Temperature Corrections", Homebrew Digest #963, 7 September 1992, https://brewery.org/library/HydromCorr0992.html.
julia> hydrometer_correction(1.046u"sg", 30u"°C")
1.0484709600383841 sg

BrewingFormulas.SUCROSE_PPG — Constant
SUCROSE_PPG

Points per pound per gallon of sucrose, 46.214: one pound of sucrose in one US gallon of solution gives a specific gravity of 1.046214.

References

  • About 46 PPG is the value commonly given for sucrose in brewing references; the source of the precise value 46.214, carried over from usual brewing software, was not found. (The extract model of this package, with SugarGravityQuad, gives about 46.18.)
